## Domain Translations

### Physics
- **Lexicon:** Finite-precision measurement (Bekenstein bound), operational QM (POVM)
- **Instance:** A Stern-Gerlach experiment: spin outcomes are always ±ℏ/2 — a rational fraction of a rational constant. No experiment has measured spin with infinite precision.
- **Ramification:** If ℚ is correct, the continuous spectrum of an operator is a limit idealization — physically, every spectrum has finite resolution. Testable: design an experiment where ℚ-based and ℝ-based physics make different predictions at some finite precision threshold.

### Computer Science
- **Lexicon:** Computable reals (Type II effectivity), approximation algorithms
- **Instance:** IEEE 754 floating-point: every computation on a physical computer is over a finite, rational subset of ℝ. The hardware physically enforces ℚ.
- **Ramification:** If physics is ℚ-based, then quantum computers (which are ℂ-based in their formalism) can only access ℚ-based outcomes — consistent with the finite precision of all real quantum measurements.

### Cognitive Science
- **Lexicon:** Just-noticeable difference (Weber-Fechner), perceptual thresholds
- **Instance:** Human perception of brightness, loudness, weight follows logarithmic or power-law psychophysical functions — we never perceive continuous gradations, only threshold crossings.
- **Ramification:** If even evolved biological sensors operate at finite resolution, the claim that physics itself operates at infinite resolution (ℝ) is an extraordinary claim — biology built sensors at the resolution physics allows.

### Information Theory
- **Lexicon:** Channel capacity, finite code length, rate-distortion
- **Instance:** The Bekenstein bound: S ≤ 2πkRE/ℏc — finite entropy in finite region → finite information. A system with finite information cannot encode an infinite-precision real number.
- **Ramification:** ℚ is the natural number system for a universe with finite information content. ℝ would require infinite information per observable — physically impossible per Bekenstein.

### Biology
- **Lexicon:** Finite genetic code (4-base alphabet), discrete mutations
- **Instance:** DNA is a finite-length quaternary code. The entire human genome is ~3.2 billion base pairs — a finite rational number of bits. Evolution operates on discrete variation.
- **Ramification:** Life — the most complex known physical system — is built on a finite, discrete code. If biology is ℚ-based, why should physics be ℝ-based?

### Sociology
- **Lexicon:** Legal codes (discrete rules), social norms (threshold-based)
- **Instance:** Law operates on discrete categories (guilty/not guilty, threshold ages, fixed penalties) — not continuous judgment spaces. Even when law uses "reasonableness" (a continuous-seeming standard), it resolves to discrete jury decisions.
- **Ramification:** Human institutions have independently converged on ℚ-like (discrete, threshold-based) decision systems. This suggests ℚ is the natural cognitive limit — and cognition is a physical process.

**Meta-Principle:** Across ALL six domains, information is fundamentally finite and discrete — infinite precision is a mathematical idealization, never a physical observable. The uncountable continuum (ℝ) is a convenient abstraction; the countable dense set (ℚ) is the physically realized structure.
